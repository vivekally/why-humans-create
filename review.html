Review Log

What the First Draft Got Wrong

Errors, omissions and unearned confidence in the earlier version of Why Do Humans Create?, what was changed, and what is still weak.

Back to the Answer Sources

An earlier version of this page was drafted by an AI model and carried its own "critical audit" tab. An independent fact-check found that the audit contained no factual corrections, claimed fixes that were not in the text, and graded a draft the reader could not see. This log replaces it. It lists what was wrong in the previous version, what was changed, and what is still weak in the version you are reading. Items are ordered by how much they would have misled a reader.

Category I

Errors and Misattributions

High

Flow was described as one of the most reliable sources of happiness

The draft said experience-sampling studies find "higher happiness during active creation than passive leisure." The best-known ESM result says almost the opposite: people are in flow more at work than at leisure and still prefer leisure (the paradox of work). Flow tracks concentration and challenge, not momentary happiness.

Changed Rewritten in Section II with the 1989 study cited. Removed from the summary cards.

High

Mumford was made to say things about startups

One sentence attributed a guild-to-factory-to-startup arc to a 1934 book. The book does not frame history as guilds versus factories, and could not have said anything about startup culture.

Changed The sentence is split. Mumford owns the historical claim; the startup extension is marked as ours.

Medium

Dawkins' extended phenotype was misapplied to human culture

The draft called human creations "an extraordinarily flexible extended phenotype." Dawkins limited the concept to artefacts under genetic influence and handled culture through memes instead.

Changed The card now states the limit.

Medium

Karma was confused with dharma

"Karma frames creation as cosmic duty" mixes two concepts. Duty is dharma. The relevant teaching for the ownership question is the Gita's nishkama karma, acting without attachment to the fruits of action, which the draft did not mention.

Changed Rewritten in Section III with the verse cited.

Medium

Psychological ownership was labelled an empirical finding

Pierce, Kostova and Dirks (2001, 2003) are theory papers. The claim that "attachment to things we've made surpasses attachment to things merely purchased" was presented as their result. It is an inference from their model that has not been tested in those terms. The draft's audit flagged William James for exactly this category error and missed the same error one card up.

Changed Relabelled as theory; the inference is marked as such.

Medium

The Kyaga aside overreached

An occupational association between creative professions and bipolar disorder was used to support "the drive to create can be compulsive, obsessive." Registry data cannot show that. The draft's audit also cited "Simonton" with no title or date and asserted burnout rates with no source.

Changed The aside now reports the 2011 and 2013 findings as associations and cites the critique of the mad-genius literature.

Low

Smaller attribution issues

Many Labs 4 was dated to its 2019 preprint; the paper was published in 2022. Pinker's byproduct list was paraphrased with terms he did not use ("pattern recognition," "emotional regulation") and given no year. The summary said SDT's third need is "connection"; the term is relatedness. The draft said the IKEA effect is "moderated by domain investment," which is not a boundary condition in the 2012 paper; completion and disassembly are.

Changed All four corrected.

Category II

Things the Draft Left Out

High

The most direct evidence on question two was missing

The draft had no Locke, so the philosophical root of "I made it, so it is mine" was absent. It had no developmental work, so it missed the finding that preschoolers assign ownership by creative labour, more strongly than adults do. It had no capuchin or child endowment-effect results, which show ownership feelings predate culture.

Changed All three added to Sections II and IV and to the summary card.

High

Startups were in the question and nowhere in the answer

The draft never engaged the entrepreneurship literature: founder passion, the "my baby" metaphor, identity-centred passion.

Changed A founders paragraph added to Section IV with the Cardon papers.

Medium

Nonhuman creation was ignored

Capuchins, chimpanzees and crows make tools. Capuchins produce stone flakes by accident, which is the strongest argument against the Lomekwi find and against any specifically human "drive to create." The draft never asked what is distinctive.

Changed Section I reframed around what is distinctive.

Medium

The music-evolution debate was twenty-five years stale

The draft presented Miller (2000) versus Pinker (1997) as the open question. The current contest is social bonding versus credible signalling, both published in 2021.

Changed Both added. Zahavi's handicap principle is now marked as itself contested.

Medium

The SDT-versus-TMT "unresolved tension" was a published debate

The draft presented approach-versus-avoidance as something "the field hasn't resolved," as though nobody had tried. Pyszczynski et al. and Ryan and Deci argued it directly in the same 2004 issue of Psychological Bulletin.

Changed The exchange is cited.

Medium

The archaeology was outdated and Neanderthals were absent

Blombos and Chauvet are no longer the interesting cases. Sulawesi, the Spanish Neanderthal marks and the Trinil shell all bear on whether the drive is a sapiens story.

Changed Added, with the Neanderthal dating marked as disputed.

Medium

The legacy motive had a better-supported account than TMT

Generativity (Erikson, McAdams) is a measured, non-defensive account of wanting one's creations to persist. The draft used only TMT.

Changed Generativity card added ahead of TMT.

Low

Legal and psychological ownership were conflated in the section meant to separate them

Open-source developers and ghostwriters were given as "creation without ownership." They usually have strong psychological ownership; what they lack is legal title.

Changed Section IV now leads with the distinction.

Category III

Problems With the Draft's Own Audit

High

It claimed fixes that were not in the text

The draft's verdict said the revision "added boundary conditions for the endowment effect." The research tab mentioned the endowment effect once, in passing, with no boundary conditions and no citation. A "non-Western frameworks" gap rated High was declared fixed by four sentences, one of which was wrong.

Changed The endowment effect has its own card with both sides of the Plott-Zeiler dispute. The non-Western section is rewritten.

High

It graded a document the reader could not see

Every item critiqued "the original," and the visible text was the revision. A reader had no way to check whether the criticisms were fair or the fixes real. The audit functioned as a signal of rigour rather than a check on it.

Changed This log states what was wrong and what was changed, and the "still weak" section below applies to the page you are reading.

Medium

It found no factual errors

Its "Factual & Attribution Issues" category contained only framing complaints. Zero wrong dates, zero misattributions. Category I above lists seven.

Medium

It softened the TMT problem while appearing to sharpen it

The draft's audit said Many Labs 4 tested worldview defence, not legacy, implying the creation claim was untouched. The creation claim rests on the same mortality-salience paradigm, so it inherits the failure rather than escaping it.

Changed Stated in the TMT card.

Low

"Authority by accumulation" was named as a flaw and then made worse

The draft's audit complained that stacking references masks weak claims, and the revision responded by adding more names. This version adds more still. The mitigation is that every name now links to a source and carries a confidence label, so the reader can weigh rather than count.

Category IV

Still Weak in This Version

Medium

The cross-cultural evidence on ownership is thin

The labour-makes-ownership rule has been tested in the UK, Japan and a few other samples. Most of the IKEA-effect and psychological-ownership work is on WEIRD samples (Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan 2010). The page says the effect "generalises" more confidently than two cultures warrant.

Open

Medium

The Henrich tension is stated, not resolved

Section I says the collective-brain view is in tension with individual-level accounts and that the two are "not simply additive." That is true and it is also a way of declining to pick. A stronger page would say which level the two questions are really asking about.

Open

Low

The non-Western traditions are still one paragraph

The section is now accurate and no longer framed as East versus West, but a paragraph is not engagement. Someone who knows the Gita commentarial tradition or Zen accounts of craft would find it thin.

Open

Low

The destruction aside has no evidence behind it

It now says so. It could be cut.

Open

Low

Sources were checked against memory and a small number of lookups, not read in full

Dates, venues and DOIs were verified for the sources marked with links. Characterisations of what each paper found are from the reviewer's knowledge of the literature and could be wrong in detail. Corrections welcome.
